Specifications and standards

What the specs on your drawing mean.

Summaries to get you oriented. They are not a substitute for the documents. Always work to the revision called out on your drawing or purchase order.

AWS D17.1Specification for Fusion Welding for Aerospace Applications

The core welding specification for aerospace hardware. It covers electron beam, laser and arc welding: procedure qualification, welder and operator qualification, weld classes (A, B and C, with A the most critical), inspection and acceptance criteria.

Weld class sets how strict the acceptance limits are. Procedures and operators must be qualified before production. Defines visual and other inspection requirements by class.

AMS 2680Electron Beam Welding for Fatigue Critical Applications

An SAE aerospace material specification for electron beam welding of parts where fatigue life matters. It sets requirements for procedure development, qualification, process control and inspection.

Tighter control for parts under cyclic loading. Qualified schedules and documented process control. Weld classes with defined acceptance limits.

AMS 2681Welding, Electron Beam

The general SAE specification for electron beam welding of aerospace parts that are not fatigue critical. Similar structure to AMS 2680 with less demanding limits.

Used when AMS 2680 is not required. Covers schedule qualification and inspection. Often called out alongside material specs on the drawing.

WS-LWS-001Weld Solutions Laser Welding Specification

Our own laser welding specification. It governs every laser weld we make unless your drawing or purchase order says otherwise, and covers procedure and operator qualification in line with AWS D17.1.

Qualified weld schedules with defined requalification limits. 100% visual inspection at 10X. Available to customers on request.

ISO 9001Quality Management Systems

The international standard for quality management systems in any industry. It focuses on understanding customer requirements, controlling processes, keeping records and continual improvement.

Applies to the whole business, not one process. Requires documented processes and records. Our quality system is built to ISO 9001:2026.

ISO 13485Medical Devices, Quality Management Systems

The quality system standard for medical device makers and their suppliers. It builds on ISO 9001 with added emphasis on risk management, process validation and traceability.

Process validation is required for special processes like welding. Strong focus on traceability and records. Common requirement from orthopedic and implant customers.

AS9100Quality Management Systems, Aviation, Space and Defense

The aerospace version of ISO 9001. It adds requirements for configuration management, product safety, counterfeit parts prevention and on time delivery.

Built on ISO 9001 with aerospace additions. Often required by aerospace primes for their suppliers. Flows down to sub tier suppliers through purchase orders.

NadcapNational Aerospace and Defense Contractors Accreditation Program

An industry run accreditation for special processes like welding, heat treating and nondestructive testing. Auditors review each process in detail against industry checklists.

Accredits a specific process, not the whole company. Required by many aerospace primes for welding suppliers. Audits are detailed and process specific.

IQ / OQ / PQInstallation, Operational and Performance Qualification

The three step validation used mostly in medical device manufacturing. IQ shows the equipment is installed correctly, OQ shows the process works across its operating window, and PQ shows it consistently makes good parts in production.

Required to validate welding under ISO 13485. OQ usually tests the edges of the parameter window. PQ runs production conditions over multiple lots.

Full documents: AWS D17.1 from the American Welding Society. AMS 2680, AMS 2681 and AS9100 from SAE International. ISO 9001 and ISO 13485 from ISO or ANSI. Nadcap checklists from PRI. Our WS-LWS-001 on request.

Glossary

Quality terms, in plain English.

Acceptance criteria
The limits a weld must meet to pass inspection, such as maximum porosity or minimum penetration.
Certificate of Conformance (C of C)
A signed statement that parts were made to the drawing, purchase order and specifications.
Conduction mode
Laser welding where heat flows in from the surface, giving shallow, wide welds with a smooth finish.
Contract review
Checking a purchase order and drawing before accepting the job, so every requirement is understood.
Cross section
A weld cut, polished and etched so penetration, width and internal quality can be measured.
Deviation
Customer approved permission to depart from a requirement for a specific job or lot.
First article
The first part made under a new or changed process, fully inspected before production continues.
Fusion zone
The part of the weld that melted and solidified.
Heat affected zone (HAZ)
Base metal next to the weld whose properties changed from heat but did not melt.
Keyhole mode
Laser or electron beam welding where the beam opens a vapor channel, giving deep, narrow welds.
Lot
A group of parts welded under the same setup, parameters and equipment.
Metlab
Short for metallurgical lab, where cross sections are prepared and examined.
Nonconformance (NCR)
A documented record of a part or process that does not meet requirements, and how it was handled.
Penetration
How deep the weld goes into the joint, measured from the surface.
Porosity
Gas pockets trapped in the weld as it solidifies.
Procedure qualification
Proof, usually by testing and sectioning, that a weld schedule produces acceptable welds.
Operator qualification
Proof that a person can run a qualified weld procedure correctly.
Special process
A process whose result cannot be fully verified by later inspection. Welding is one.
Traceability
The ability to link a part back to its material, machine, parameters, operator and date.
Weld class
A category, such as Class A, B or C in AWS D17.1, that sets how strict inspection is.
Weld schedule
The recorded parameters, fixturing and steps for welding a specific part.
